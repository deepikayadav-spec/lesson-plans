## Learning Objectives

By the end of this session, students will be able to:

1. State that when all partners invest for the same period, profit is shared strictly in the ratio of their capital. *(REMEMBERING)*
2. Explain why two partners who invest equal capital for different durations cannot receive equal profit shares. *(UNDERSTANDING)*
3. Compute each partner's profit share from a given set of capitals (two or three partners) using the ratio-of-investment method. *(APPLYING)*
4. Apply the capital × time rule to find a partner's share when an investment changes mid-year. *(APPLYING)*
5. Solve partnership problems where a fixed amount is deducted from each partner's share before the remainder is split in a given ratio. *(APPLYING)*
6. Reverse-engineer the investment ratio of partners from a given profit-sharing ratio and their respective investment durations. *(ANALYZING)*

## Warm-Up Poll — Prior Knowledge Activation (3–7 min) · ALS: Polling

5 questions on **Session 21 — Mixtures and Alligations**. Newly authored, grounded in that deck's real numbers. ~45 s each, project the distribution, never name individuals.

**Q1.** What is the name of the method used to find the mixing ratio when two ingredients of different cost or quality are combined to make a mixture of some intermediate value?
`A` Alligation rule · `B` Permutation rule · `C` Probability rule · `D` HCF rule
→ **A.**

**Q2.** Two varieties of pulses cost Rs.15/kg and Rs.20/kg. They are mixed to get a mixture worth Rs.16.50/kg. What is the mixing ratio (cheaper : dearer)?
`A` 3:7 · `B` 7:3 · `C` 1:2 · `D` 2:1
→ **B.**

**Q3.** Water is mixed with milk costing Rs.12/litre to get a mixture worth Rs.8/litre. In the alligation calculation, what price is water taken to be?
`A` Rs.8/litre · `B` Rs.4/litre · `C` Rs.0/litre · `D` Rs.12/litre
→ **C.** *If >40% miss this,* re-run the water/milk alligation cross on the board before moving on.

**Q4.** A shopkeeper has 1000 kg of sugar. Part is sold at 8% profit, the rest at 18% profit, and he gains 14% overall. How much was sold at 18% profit?
`A` 400 kg · `B` 500 kg · `C` 600 kg · `D` 700 kg
→ **C.**

**Q5.** *(MSQ — select all that apply)* Which of these ratio answers from last session are correct?
`A` Pulses (Rs.15 & Rs.20) → mixture at Rs.16.50 gives ratio 7:3 · `B` Water:Milk (Rs.12/L) → mixture at Rs.8/L gives ratio 1:2 · `C` Sugar (Rs.30 & Rs.40/kg) sold at Rs.36/kg with 10% gain gives ratio 8:3 · `D` Rice (Rs.20/kg) mixed with rice (Rs.30/kg) to get Rs.26/kg gives ratio 3:2
→ **A, B, C.** *(D is false — the real answer is 2:3.)* *Targets:* whether students remember which side of the ratio belongs to which ingredient — the same direction-of-ratio care you'll need today when assigning shares to named partners.

**Running it** — poll tool, ~45 s per question. Total ~3.75 min for the 5 questions.

## Hook (7–9 min)

Say this, writing the numbers on the board as you go:

> *"Two of your friends decide to open a food stall together. Friend A puts in Rs.1,20,000. Friend B puts in Rs.1,50,000. At the end of the year, they've made a profit of Rs.27,000. Show of hands — who thinks they should just split it fifty-fifty, half each?"*

Let hands go up. Take one "yes" and one "no" reason out loud.

> *"Real partnerships almost never split it evenly. The size of your investment decides your share — and, as you'll see in about ten minutes, so does something most people forget entirely: how long you kept that money in the business. Yesterday you used ratios to mix pulses and sugar profits. Today the exact same ratio tool decides how business partners split real money."*

## Slide Block A (9–15 min) — DELIVER SLIDES AS-IS

<!-- placement: inferred — the deck's slide-by-slide timing isn't tagged in the text extraction; boundary chosen so Block A ends on the capital×time concept before students practise it themselves -->

Covers: Partnerships title/agenda slide → Example 1 (two partners, equal time) → Example 2 (three partners, equal time) → Example 3 (capital changes mid-year, with a partial withdrawal).

**Beats to emphasise**

- **Beat 1 — Equal time, equal treatment.** A and B invest Rs.1,20,000 and Rs.1,50,000 for the full year, profit Rs.27,000. Ratio 4:5, unit=3000. **A = Rs.12,000, B = Rs.15,000.** This resolves the Hook.
- **Beat 2 — Same idea, three partners.** A, B, C invest Rs.1,20,000, Rs.1,80,000, Rs.1,50,000, profit Rs.27,000. Ratio 4:6:5, unit=1800. **A=Rs.7,200, B=Rs.10,800, C=Rs.9,000.**
- **Beat 3 — The rule breaks if time isn't equal.** A invests Rs.1,00,000 for 12 months. B invests Rs.2,00,000 after 3 months (9 months), then withdraws half after month 9 (Rs.1,00,000 for the last 3 months). C invests Rs.3,00,000 after 9 months (3 months). Annual profit Rs.1,20,000. Find B's share.
  - A: `1,00,000×12=12,00,000`. B: `(2,00,000×6)+(1,00,000×3)=15,00,000`. C: `3,00,000×3=9,00,000`. Total=36,00,000.
  - B's share = `1,20,000 × 15,00,000/36,00,000 = `**Rs.50,000.**
  - *"Profit share depends on capital **and** time invested — not capital alone."*

**Checkpoint (at 15 min)** — 10 s silent think, cold-call:
> *"A friend says: 'B put in double A's capital at some point, so B should always get double A's share.' True here?"*
> **Answer:** No — B's effective capital×time (15,00,000) isn't double A's (12,00,000), so the share isn't double either.

## Quiz Time Walkthrough (15–22 min) — deck's own MCQs, live vote-then-reveal

> **Classroom Quiz: not yet available.** This block substitutes a live walkthrough of the deck's own "Quiz Time" MCQs — cold-call, poll for A/B/C/D, then reveal and explain. Not a platform bank, but the closest grounded substitute.

**Q1** *(deck slide 11)* A and B invest in a business in the ratio 3:2. 5% of the total profit goes to charity, and A's share is Rs.855. What is the total profit?
- A) 1425 · B) **1500** · C) 1537.5 · D) 1576

> **Working:** Only 95% of the profit is shared. A's fraction of the *total* = 0.95 × 3/5 = 0.57. Total = 855 ÷ 0.57 = **Rs.1,500.**
> **If they pick A) 1425:** they divided 855 by 3/5 alone and forgot the 5%-to-charity deduction.

**Q2** *(deck slide 12)* A, B, C together subscribe Rs.50,000. A subscribes Rs.4,000 more than B, and B subscribes Rs.5,000 more than C. Out of a total profit of Rs.35,000, what does A receive?
- A) 8400 · B) 11900 · C) 13600 · D) **14700**

> **Working:** Let C=x. B=x+5000, A=x+9000. `3x+14000=50000 → x=12000`. Ratio 21:17:12, sum 50. A's share = `35000×21/50=`**Rs.14,700.**
> **If they pick C or B:** they likely set up the offsets in the wrong direction.

**Debrief line:**
> *"Notice both of these still come back to the same rule — figure out what fraction of the total profit is actually being shared, and what ratio it's shared in. Everything else is bookkeeping."*

## Slide Block B (22–28 min) — DELIVER SLIDES AS-IS

<!-- placement: inferred — this is the last substantive teaching slide before the deck's closing "Quiz Time" divider, feedback, and thank-you slides -->

Covers: the reverse problem — given the **profit ratio** and each partner's **investment duration**, find the **investment ratio**.

**Beat to emphasise**

- **Running the rule backwards.** Three partners share profit in the ratio 5:7:8. They invested for 14, 8, and 7 months respectively. Since Profit ratio = Investment ratio × Time ratio (forward direction), the reverse must **divide**, not multiply: Investment ratio = Profit ratio ÷ Time ratio.
  - A: 5/14 · B: 7/8 · C: 8/7. LCM(14,8,7)=56.
  - A: 20 · B: 49 · C: 64 → **Investment ratio = 20:49:64.**
- **Verify it live:** multiply back — 20×14=280, 49×8=392, 64×7=448 — divide through by 56, land back on 5:7:8. *"That check is what tells you the answer is right — always run it if you have the time."*

**Checkpoint (at 28 min)** — show hands:
> *"If I'd multiplied the profit ratio by the time instead of dividing, would I get a bigger or smaller number for A compared to 20?"*
> **Answer:** Bigger — multiplying instead of dividing by the time ratio inflates every term.

## ⚡ ALS Activity 1 — Spot the Bug (28–34 min)

**ALS format:** Silent Diagnose, Named Reveal — a worked solution with a deliberate, realistic error goes on the board, and students must find *what's wrong with the method*, not just disagree with the number. Chosen because this is the session's single most important misconception, and the deck itself flags it directly.

**Setup line:**
> *"This is a real answer someone gave for the mid-year problem from Slide Block A — A invests Rs.1,00,000 for 12 months, B invests Rs.2,00,000 then withdraws half after month 9, C invests Rs.3,00,000 for the last 3 months, annual profit Rs.1,20,000. Find the flaw."*

**The buggy solution:**
> *"A invested Rs.1,00,000. B invested Rs.2,00,000. C invested Rs.3,00,000. Ratio = 1:2:3, sum = 6. B's share = 1,20,000 × 2/6 = Rs.40,000."*

**The flaw:** the "solution" uses only the capital amounts and ignores how long each amount was actually invested. Using capital×time (Slide Block A), B's correct total is 15,00,000 against a grand total of 36,00,000, giving **Rs.50,000** — not Rs.40,000.

> *"Rs.40,000 is what you get when you split by capital alone. Rs.50,000 is what you get when you split by capital **and** time. That ten-thousand-rupee gap is the whole lesson of today's session."*

**When it goes wrong**

| If… | Do this |
|---|---|
| Students say "the ratio 1:2:3 is just wrong" without saying why | Push: *"Wrong because of what, specifically?"* Make them name capital×time. |
| Someone defends Rs.40,000 | Ask them to recompute A's capital×time (12,00,000) and B's raw capital before the withdrawal (also 12,00,000 for 6 months) — suspiciously equal, which is the giveaway. |

**Debrief line:**
> *"Let students name the flaw themselves before you re-derive it — that's the point of the activity."*

**Cut rule:** Skip the "When it goes wrong" extensions and go straight from reveal to the debrief line.

## ⚡ ALS Activity 2 — Trace the Table (34–41 min)

**ALS format:** Guided Table Build — the class rebuilds, row by row on the board, the profit→investment reverse-calculation from Slide Block B, but this time *they* call out each cell. Chosen as the closing activity because Slide Block B was delivered as a worked example — this is the only chance to run the reverse-ratio method with their own hands.

**Setup line:**
> *"Same problem as Slide Block B — profit ratio 5:7:8, months 14, 8, 7 — but this time I'm not solving it. You call out every cell, one at a time, and I write exactly what you say."*

Draw an empty table: Partner | Profit ratio | Time (months) | Profit ÷ Time | ×56 (LCM) | Investment ratio.

**The table, completed**

| Partner | Profit ratio | Time (months) | Profit ÷ Time | × 56 | Investment ratio |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| A | 5 | 14 | 5/14 | 20 | **20** |
| B | 7 | 8 | 7/8 | 49 | **49** |
| C | 8 | 7 | 8/7 | 64 | **64** |

**When it goes wrong**

| If… | Do this |
|---|---|
| Someone calls out Profit × Time instead of Profit ÷ Time | Expected trap — point back at Slide Block B's checkpoint: "which direction does the arrow run when we reverse it?" |
| Class gets stuck finding the LCM of 14, 8, 7 | Build it together: 14=2×7, 8=2³, 7=7 → LCM = 2³×7 = 56. |

**Debrief line:**
> *"Rotate who calls out each row — the whole room stays accountable, not just one confident voice."*

**Cut rule:** Do only the Profit ÷ Time column live and state the final ×56 ratio yourself.

## Exit Ticket + Homework (46–48 min)

**Exit ticket** (~1 min) — before anyone leaves:

> In today's mid-year investment problem, B's correct share is Rs.50,000 — not Rs.40,000. In one or two sentences, explain what mistake produces Rs.40,000, and why it's wrong.
> **Answer:** Rs.40,000 comes from splitting by raw capital alone (ratio 1:2:3, ignoring time). It's wrong because B did not keep the full Rs.2,00,000 invested for the whole year — share must be based on capital × time, not capital alone.

Scan responses on the way out. Any answer that doesn't name *time* as the missing factor is the signal to reopen this example at the start of Session 23.

**Homework**

1. Re-solve, from scratch, both Quiz Time walkthrough problems (charity-deduction, subscribe-capital-with-offsets).
2. Attempt this new deduction problem independently: *"Rs.5,783 is divided among A, B, C such that if Rs.28, Rs.37, Rs.18 are deducted from their shares, the remainder is in ratio 4:6:9. Find A's share."* (Total deducted=83, remainder=5700, unit=300, A's remainder-share=1200, **A's full share = Rs.1,228**.)
3. If setting up ratios quickly still feels shaky, revisit Session 21's alligation examples.

> *"Tonight, before you touch any of these, write down the one rule from today in your own words: profit share depends on capital *and* time."*

## Common Misconceptions

| Misconception | Why students hold it | Correct it live by |
|---|---|---|
| Profit is split by capital alone, ignoring how long each amount was invested | It's the simpler rule, correct whenever time happens to be equal | ALS Activity 1 — the Rs.40,000 vs Rs.50,000 comparison |
| A late-joining or part-withdrawn investment counts for the full period anyway | Students track *how much* money was put in, not *when* | Slide Block A, Beat 3 |
| In a deduction-then-ratio problem, the final share is just the ratio share of the remainder | The ratio step is the "interesting" part, so the add-back step gets skipped | Homework problem 2 — catching the "forgot to add back" version |
| Reversing "profit ratio → investment ratio" means multiplying by time again | The forward rule multiplies, so reversing feels like it should too | Slide Block B's live verification step |
| The full profit is always split among the named partners | Deductions aren't mentioned unless the problem states them | Quiz Time Walkthrough Q1 — "only 95% is shared" |
