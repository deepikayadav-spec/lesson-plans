## Learning Objectives

By the end of this session, students will be able to:

1. Define aptitude and state how it differs from general mathematics ability. *(REMEMBERING)*
2. List at least three real-world contexts where aptitude is tested — recruitment drives, qualifying exams, government exams. *(REMEMBERING)*
3. Explain why aptitude is assessed so widely, using the deck's own "Applications" categories (financial services, technology & engineering, R&D, personal development). *(UNDERSTANDING)*
4. Describe the structure of this Aptitude course — its four components and its delivery format (sessions, skill assessments, module quizzes, practice sessions, study material). *(REMEMBERING)*
5. State what they should walk away with by the end of the course, per the deck's "Achievements" slide. *(UNDERSTANDING)*
6. Apply an assume-and-adjust reasoning strategy to solve a classic heads-and-legs riddle. *(APPLYING)*

## Warm-Up Poll — Diagnostic (3–7 min) · ALS: Polling

> **This session is the exception.** There is no previous session to recall, so the poll is a *diagnostic*, not retrieval practice. No wrong answers. Purpose is to calibrate pace and establish that this classroom expects everyone to answer.

Say: *"Five quick questions. Nobody is graded, nobody is named. I need to know who I'm teaching."*

**Q1.** Have you attempted an aptitude test before — school, coaching, mock test, recruitment, anything?
`A` Never · `B` Once or twice · `C` Several times · `D` Regularly practice
→ *Read:* If A+B > 60%, this is genuinely day one for most of the room — go slower through the "Extent of Aptitude" slides, don't assume familiarity with the company/exam names.

**Q2.** In your own words — what's the difference between "aptitude" and "maths," if any?
`A` No difference, same thing · `B` Aptitude is maths done faster · `C` Aptitude tests reasoning and thinking, not just calculation · `D` Not sure
→ *Read:* C is where today lands. If very few pick C, that's exactly the gap this session closes — don't correct it now, let Slide Block A do it.

**Q3.** How comfortable are you solving a puzzle or riddle under a little time pressure?
`A` Very uncomfortable · `B` A bit nervous · `C` Fairly comfortable · `D` I enjoy it
→ *Read:* Calibrates the energy you bring into ALS Activity 2 (the riddle) later in the session.

**Q4.** Which of these have you already heard of? *(MSQ — pick as many as apply)*
`A` TCS / Infosys / Wipro-type company drives · `B` CAT / GATE / GRE-type entrance exams · `C` UPSC / SSC / bank-type government exams · `D` None of these
→ *Read:* Tells you how much of the Extent-of-Aptitude lists will land as "new information" versus "confirmation." If D is high, slow down there.

**Q5.** How do you prefer a session like this to run?
`A` Mostly listen, take notes · `B` Discuss in pairs or groups · `C` Solve problems live, out loud · `D` A mix of all three
→ *Read:* If C/D dominate, lean harder into the activities and keep the lecture stretches tight.

**Running it** — poll tool, ~45 s per question. Total ~3.75 min for the 5 questions.

## Hook (7–10 min)

Say: *"Before I show you the next slide — guess. Out of the top IT recruiters in India, how many do you think run an aptitude round before they even look at your resume?"*

Take two or three shouted guesses. Write the numbers on the board.

Now reveal the deck's "Extent of Aptitude — Recruitment drives" slide: **TCS, IBM, Cognizant, HCL, Mindtree, Capgemini, Accenture, Infosys, Deloitte, Dell, DXC, Wipro, Tech Mahindra, Hexaware, ADP, EY, Amdocs, Atos, HP, Mphasis, VMware, Oracle, Zoho, L&T, Genpact.**

> *"Twenty-five companies on this one slide alone — and this is one of four categories we're about to look at. Every single one of them puts an aptitude round before the technical interview. Not after. Before."*

Tie back to **Q4** of the poll: *"Some of you already recognised half this list. By the end of today you'll know exactly why it's there."*

## ⚡ ALS Activity 1 — Real-World Callout (19–25 min)

**ALS format:** Open-Floor Callout — students shout out any company, exam, or government test they personally know someone who has taken an aptitude round for, not restricted to the deck's lists. Chosen as the first activity because the poll and hook already established that aptitude testing is widespread in the abstract; this makes it personal before the session moves into course logistics.

**Setup line:**
> *"Thirty seconds — call out any company, exam, or government test you personally know someone who's taken an aptitude round for. Doesn't have to be from our list. I'll write it under the right category."*

Have the "Extent of Aptitude" lists visible on screen or written as headers on the board, ready to add student callouts underneath.

**How it surfaces:** For 2–3 of the callouts, push once: *"What do you know about that round — was it timed, was it online, did they say what it covered?"* Accept anything students actually know; you're building evidence, not testing them.

**Debrief line:**
> *"Notice something — every single callout you just gave me lands in one of these four categories. This isn't a syllabus topic invented for a classroom. It's a filter used everywhere, and you've already brushed up against it."*

**Cut rule:** Cap it at 5 callouts total and skip the push-for-detail step. Do not cut the debrief line.

## ⚡ ALS Activity 2 — Silent Solve → Vote-Lock → Reveal: The Goats-and-Hens Riddle (32–40 min)

**ALS format:** Silent Solve, Vote-Lock, Then Reveal — students commit to an answer letter before anything is explained, locking in a public guess before the technique is shown. **This is the deck's only worked problem and the session's one genuine problem-solving moment — protect this slot above everything else if the session runs behind.**

**Setup line:**
> *"Forty-three heads, one hundred twenty-six legs, some goats, some hens. A) 26, B) 32, C) 20, D) 23. Pick one before I show you anything. Two minutes — work it out alone or with your neighbour."*

Give 2 minutes silent/paired work, then take a show-of-hands vote on A/B/C/D before revealing anything.

After the vote:
> *"Now watch how the slide does it. Assume, just for a second, that every single animal is a hen."*

**The reveal (exactly as the deck presents it):**
1. **Assume all 43 are hens.** Total legs would be 43 × 2 = **86**.
2. **But the question says there are 126 legs.** Why the difference?
3. **Because some of them are goats!** The gap is 126 − 86 = **40 extra legs**.
4. Each goat contributes 2 more legs than a hen would (4 − 2). So the number of goats = 40 ÷ 2 = **20**.

**Answer: C) 20 goats** (and 23 hens). Verify out loud: 20 goats × 4 legs = 80, plus 23 hens × 2 legs = 46, total = 126 legs; 20 + 23 = 43 heads. ✔

**Debrief line:**
> *"No algebra, no equations — just 'assume the simple case, then measure the gap.' That's the kind of technique this course is built on. You'll use some version of this trick constantly."*

**Cut rule:** If running short, skip the show-of-hands vote and go straight to the reveal, but keep the "assume all hens" framing intact — that framing *is* the technique being taught.

## Common Misconceptions

| Misconception | Why students hold it | Correct it live by |
|---|---|---|
| "Aptitude is just another maths subject" | School experience only tested calculation, never framed reasoning-under-time separately | Slide Block A's explicit aptitude-vs-maths beat, reinforced by poll Q2 |
| "Aptitude only matters for IT company drives" | Recruitment drives are the most visible/talked-about category | ALS Activity 1 and the full four-category spread in Slide Block A |
| "A riddle like this needs algebra to solve properly" | School conditioning that word problems require setting up equations | ALS Activity 2's reveal — the deck's own assume-and-adjust technique, no equations |
| "This course is about calculating fast" | "Aptitude" sounds speed-focused | Slide Block B's Achievements beat — "methodologies & approaches," not just speed |
| "I've never really seen an aptitude question before" | Students don't recognise school/mock-test puzzles as "aptitude" until named as such | Warm-up poll Q1/Q4 usually surfaces prior exposure most students didn't label correctly |
